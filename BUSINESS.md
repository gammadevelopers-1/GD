## 1. Positioning

> **Most businesses don't have a growth problem. They have a leak problem.**
> The revenue was already paid for. It's leaking out of the gaps between the tools, the people, and the hours nobody's working.

We are not a "we build software" agency. We are the people who find where money is falling out of a business and plug it.

Three pillars everything ladders to:

**1. Leaks are invisible because nobody owns them.**
Every leak sits *between* two job descriptions. The marketer's job ends when the form submits. The salesperson's job starts when the lead is "assigned." Nobody's KPI covers the gap in between, so nobody sees it.

**2. The fix is an overlay, not a replacement.**
This is the single most important line we have. Owners have been burned by "let's migrate everything to a new system" projects. Our promise: your team keeps working exactly how they work today. We add a layer on top that catches what falls through. Nothing gets ripped out. Nothing gets re-learned.

**3. They already paid for what they're losing.**
The lead cost ad spend. The invoice typing costs salary. The dormant list cost money to build. This isn't new spend for new results — it's recovering spend already made. That reframe is what makes the price feel small.

**The flywheel:** plug the leak → hours come back → the team serves clients better → clients stay and refer → more volume → more leaks worth plugging.

## 2. Who we target

An **owner or ops lead at a 10–200 person business**, GCC and US.

- Time-poor, doing eight things at once
- Already been pitched by three agencies
- Allergic to jargon
- Not technical — doesn't know or care what the stack is
- Thinks in money, time, headcount, and "who's dropping the ball"

**First slice we're working:** skin clinics and beauty studios in the UAE, 5–50 staff.

**Their awareness level — this matters more than anything else:**

| They know | They don't know |
|---|---|
| They're busy and something's off | That it has a name |
| Leads "don't convert" | That the leads were fine and the callback was late |
| Someone spends all Monday on invoices | What that costs per year |
| They need to hire | That the role they're hiring for is mostly typing |

They are **problem-aware but not solution-aware.** They cannot search for what we sell, because they don't know it exists. So we never open with the solution. **Our job is to name the problem for them.**

**Recurring failure mode to avoid:** techy aesthetic and techy language aimed at a non-technical SMB owner. If a sentence only lands with a developer, it's wrong.

## 3. What we actually provide

Five leaks. Always described as the symptom, never the mechanism.

| System | What the owner says | Where the money goes |
|---|---|---|
| **Speed to Lead** | "We get plenty of leads, they just don't convert." | Between form submit and first human contact. The lead didn't go cold, it went to whoever answered first. |
| **Document Processing** | "Someone spends Monday retyping invoices into a sheet." | Salary spent on typing, plus late fees, missed discounts, and one person who can't take leave. |
| **Follow-Up** | "We got 150 registrations and then… I don't know what happened." | Between "showed interest" and "written off." The warmest list they own. |
| **Database Reactivation** | "We've got 8,000 contacts in the CRM." | Between acquisition and abandonment. All paid for, all sitting there, while ad spend goes up. |
| **Internal Reporting** | "Friday disappears into building the same report." | Senior people doing assembly work. This one leaks decision quality, not just hours. |

**Above these** sit full custom platforms — but that's what we grow into, not what we open with.

**How we enter:**

```
AI Opportunity Audit  →  One system, prove the number  →  Expand  →  Platform / retainer
     (the door)              (fast, measurable)            (2-3 more)     (where MRR lives)
```

**Sequencing rule:** lead with speed-to-lead or document processing — acute, obvious, fast to prove. Then reactivation (quick revenue, buys goodwill), then follow-up, then reporting.

**Reporting is the land-and-expand play.** To build the report you have to touch every system in the business. That's paid discovery that surfaces every other leak. *Sell reporting, discover everything else.*

## 4. Our selling points

What we say when they ask "why you."

**1. We prove the number before we ask for the budget.**
The audit produces their arithmetic, not our brochure. Most agencies open with a scope and a price. We open with what one specific leak is costing them this year.

**2. Nothing gets replaced.**
Their systems stay. Their team doesn't learn a new tool. This kills the biggest objection before it's raised.

**3. We compete with a salary, not with software.**
Almost every one of these leaks is currently on the roadmap to be fixed by hiring someone. That's the comparator. Never anchor against a monthly SaaS subscription.

**4. We say what automation can't do.**
Humans review the exceptions. Formats drift. It will make mistakes, and so does the manual process — the difference is we measure ours. Operators trust the person who says this out loud.

**5. We've actually shipped this.**
Real delivered platforms with named clients, including a beauty/clinic group running its whole operation on what we built. Proof of work beats every pitch deck. Never invent a client, a metric, or a testimonial — pull from real projects or ask.

## 6. How we talk

**Rule one: symptom first, solution last.** Recognition → cost → fix.

**Banned:** revolutionize · game-changer · 10x · unlock · seamless · cutting-edge · AI-powered (as an adjective) · transform your business · leverage · synergy. Also: em dashes, journaling tone.

**Never name a tool in owner-facing copy.** Tool names invite "we already have X" and turn a value conversation into a procurement conversation.

| Don't say | Say |
|---|---|
| Workflow automation | Nobody has to do that step anymore |
| Integration | The two systems stop needing a person in between |
| Webhook / trigger | It happens the moment the form is submitted |
| Human-in-the-loop | Your team reviews the exceptions, not the routine |
| AI agent | It reads it, pulls out what matters, flags what's unclear |
| Reduce operational overhead | Give someone their Friday back |
| Scalable | It costs the same at 500 invoices as at 200 |

**Say often:** Your systems stay. Your team doesn't learn a new tool. We start with one leak, prove the number, then expand. Humans stay where judgment matters.

**Never say:** "Replace your team." "Fully autonomous." Guaranteed conversion percentages. Anything implying their current setup is stupid — it was rational when it was built.

## 7. The math is the pitch

Owners believe their own arithmetic more than anyone's research.

```
Fully-loaded hourly cost = (salary + benefits + overhead) ÷ working hours
                         ≈ 1.25–1.4× base salary   ← use this, not base pay

Repetitive task   = volume × minutes each × loaded rate × 12
Slow response     = leads past the window × close-rate delta × deal value
Dormant list      = dormant contacts × reply rate × close rate × deal value
Manual reporting  = hours/week × 52 × loaded rate
```

**Convert hours into headcount.** "50 hours a month" is abstract. "A third of a person, permanently, doing data entry" is a decision.

## 8. Stat integrity (hard rule)

Reputation is the whole asset. One debunked stat costs more than the post earned.

**Cite freely, always with the year:**
- Oldroyd / InsideSales Lead Response Management, **2007** — 5 vs 30 minutes changed odds of *making contact* ~100x, odds of *qualifying* ~21x.
- HBR **2011**, 2,241 US firms — ~42-hour average first response time.
- Ardent Partners AP benchmarks, **2025** — cost per invoice ~$9–13 average vs ~$2.78 best-in-class.

**Use as ranges only, as practitioner experience:** reactivation engagement on dormant lists (low single digits), touches to close, any conversion-lift figure.

**Never use:** "80% of sales require 5 follow-ups." "1,200% ROI on reactivation." "78% buy from whoever responds first." Any percentage without a nameable study, year, and sample.

**Rules:** If you can't name the study, don't publish the number. Old studies are fine, say the year out loud. Odds of *contact* ≠ odds of *sale*. When in doubt, replace the stat with arithmetic — arithmetic can't be debunked.
